## B. Context: the last 12 months for risk assets

**Rates shock, not growth shock.** The defining macro fact at the start date is a Fed that is
raising rates into an equity market at record highs. The 16 Sep hike was unanimous and the
statement framed it as insurance against inflation broadening out of energy [17][19]. Warsh:
"This summer's inflation readings do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully
improved" [19]. The 10-year broke 5.1% on 23 Sep after hot S&P Global PMIs (fastest private-
sector expansion in 5+ years, with rising cost pressures), hawkish Fed commentary, a weak
5-year auction and elevated oil [11][12][13]. Curve steepening in 2026 has been led by the
long end (term premium), which commentators read as an inflation/supply signal rather than a
recession signal [16]. Futures now price ~4.7% fed funds by Sep 2027, roughly 85 bp above the
current midpoint (*computed*) [24].

**Oil and the Iran conflict.** US strikes on Iran in late May 2026 revived Strait of Hormuz
fears [66]; a 60-day ceasefire expired in mid-August; strikes on tankers by both sides
intensified in early September, with Hormuz traffic down to ~10 commodity ships/day and Brent
near $97 [40][41][67]. Iran and Oman were reported to be finalising a temporary safe route
through the Strait to register with the IMO [40]. Oil near $100 fed the 3.4% headline CPI
print [30][68]. Start date of the conflict: [NOT FOUND] in this session's results.

**Bitcoin: cycle top, crash, partial recovery.** BTC set its ATH of ~$126.2k on 6 Oct 2025,
~534 days after the April 2024 halving, in line with the 525–549-day intervals of the two prior
cycles [45][46]. On 10 Oct 2025 a single-day cascade produced ~$19B of liquidations, the
largest on record [69][70]. BTC closed 2025 near $88.4k [48], fell to ~$58k in June 2026
(−54% from ATH, a shallower bear than prior cycles' 75%+) [46], and traded ~$63–65k in
mid-July [46][71]. Since then it has rallied to ~$85k on ETF inflows and a record short
squeeze on 20 Aug ($2.74B of shorts liquidated, the largest short-side wipe-out ever) [72].
Other 2026 liquidation events: "Black Sunday II" on 1 Feb ($2.2B, ETH longs $961M; gold and
silver crashed the same day) [73]; a January cascade of $1.7B as the crypto fear index hit a
2026 low [74]; and the 4–6 June cascade (>$3B) [47].

**AI capex cycle.** 2026 guidance: Amazon ~$200B, Alphabet $175–185B, Meta $115–135B,
Microsoft ~$120B+, Oracle ~$50B; the big four together ~$725B and the top five $660–690B
depending on tracker, roughly double 2025 [75][76][77]. For 2027, Evercore and BofA project
combined big-tech capex above $1T; one tracker has Google $284.8B, Amazon $256.5B, Microsoft
$207.6B, Meta $185.6B (total $934.5B); Goldman has $1.01T for 2027 vs $765B for 2026 (+32%)
[78][79]. Amazon says capacity constraints persist through 2027 [77]. Investor tolerance is
thinning: an Alphabet capex report in late July triggered a sell-off and higher scrutiny of
the other hyperscalers [80]. NVIDIA's revenue run-rate (~$385B annualised, *computed* from
[62]) is the direct read-through.

**Bubble talk.** Strategists are explicitly comparing 2026 to 1999: a handful of AI mega-caps
exceed 30% of the S&P 500 and CAPE is ~41 vs 44 at the 1999 peak [8][9][81][82]. Counter-
argument (Goldman, JPM): hyperscaler capex is cash-flow funded, not debt/equity funded, and
monetisation has begun [82]. Named "would-plunge" cohorts if the cycle turns: neoclouds
(CoreWeave, Nebius), hardware assemblers (Super Micro) [83]. Tech led "the worst sell-off since
October" at one point in 2026, with AI mega-caps losing hundreds of billions in a session
(date not given in summary) [82].

**IPO market: record, driven by three names.** US IPO proceeds YTD are a record $146B, of
which SpaceX's June IPO was $75B (the largest ever); ex-SpaceX proceeds are $71B; the
Renaissance IPO Index is +15% YTD; Renaissance's 8 Sep fall preview flagged Anthropic and
OpenAI as possible autumn listings [84][85]. SpaceX priced at ~$1.77T, ran to ~$2.5T on a
4% float, and has since fallen to ~$1.4T after its first earnings report [86]. Anthropic filed
a confidential S-1 on 1 June after a $65B Series H at $965B post-money; OpenAI announced its
filing on 8 June and has discussed a round at ~$1.2T [86][87]. Together the three exceed $5T,
more than the combined debut value of all 3,365 tech IPOs of the prior 45 years ($4.1T, per Jay
Ritter data cited by the FT) [86]. Read-through: enormous prospective supply of AI equity.

**Blow-ups in 2025–26: private credit and fraud.** Tricolor (subprime auto, Ch.7, 10 Sep 2025)
and First Brands (auto parts, Ch.11, 28 Sep 2025) collapsed amid fraud; executives of both were
criminally charged (First Brands in late Jan 2026) [88][89]. Saks Fifth Avenue filed in early
Jan 2026 with >$3B of debt [90]. UK lender Market Financial Solutions defaulted amid
"double-pledging" allegations; Apollo, Ares and Blue Owl restricted investor withdrawals [88][89].
372 large US companies filed for bankruptcy in H1 2026, the most for a first half since 2010
[91]. This is the credit-side stress to monitor if rates stay >5%.

**Politics and trade.** Midterms are on 3 Nov 2026. Senate is 53R–47D with 35 seats up (22 R);
Democrats need a net +4 and lead polling averages in the six likeliest tipping-point states;
the generic ballot is D+8.9, the largest Democratic lead of the cycle, with fuel costs cited as
a driver [92][93]. On tariffs: the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs 6–3 on 20 Feb 2026
(Learning Resources v. Trump) [94]; the administration replaced them the same day with a 10%
Section 122 global surcharge, which lapsed at its 150-day statutory limit on 24 Jul 2026 and was
immediately replaced by Section 301 duties of 10–12.5% on 60 economies (forced-labour basis)
[95][96][97]. Section 232 duties remain (50% steel/aluminium for most countries, 25% copper)
[98]. Net effect: trade-weighted average US tariff ~11.2%, barely changed, but the structure is
now country-specific and litigable [96].

**Recession indicators: not signalling.** August 2026 payrolls +162k (vs +55k expected),
unemployment 4.1% unchanged, U-6 7.7%, nonfarm employment at an all-time high [99][100].
Sahm rule −0.03 (trigger +0.50), down from a 0.1 peak in early July [101]. Q2 2026 real GDP
+1.5% annualised (second estimate, 26 Aug), consumer spending +3.4% [102]. The 3m/10y curve is
positively sloped given fed funds 3.75–4.00% and 10y 5.11% (*computed*). The growth risk is
therefore a *policy-induced* slowdown from here, not one already visible in the data.

**Liquidity regime.** QT ended 1 Dec 2025 and the balance sheet has drifted up to $6.75T with
reserves ~$3.0T ("ample") [27][28][29]; one source reports a record global M2 in Feb 2026, but
the quoted figure ($22.67T) looks like US M2 rather than global, so it is not relied on [27].
Net: quantity of money is not tightening, but its *price* is rising fast (5.1% 10y, hikes
priced), the dollar is at a 2-month high, and margin debt is ~$1.45T (see D). That is a
"tight-price, ample-quantity" regime: hostile to duration and to unprofitable growth, less
hostile to cash-generative names, and the ETF bid has kept crypto liquid despite it.

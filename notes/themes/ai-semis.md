## 1. Why this theme could produce a 10x in 1-2 years

**The capital flow is real and still accelerating in nominal terms.** The four US hyperscalers
(Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta) plan roughly $725bn of 2026 capex, up ~77% on 2025's ~$410bn
[1]. Company guidance as summarised: Amazon ~$200bn, Alphabet raised to $180-190bn, Meta raised to
$125-145bn [2][3][4]. Third-party forecasts for 2027 sit at ~$935bn for the big four (Google $284.8bn,
Amazon $256.5bn, Microsoft $207.6bn, Meta $185.6bn) [8], with "$1tn in 2027" used as the round number
[1][9]; Morgan Stanley's broader data-centre capex number is $1.3tn for 2027 and $1.5tn for 2028 [7].

**NVIDIA's last print shows no demand saturation yet.** Q2 FY2027 (quarter ended 26 Jul 2026):
revenue $96.2bn, +18% q/q, +106% y/y; Data Center $89.0bn, +117% y/y; GAAP and non-GAAP gross
margin 75.0%; Q3 FY2027 revenue guidance $108.0bn [10][11]. On the call Huang forecast ~70% revenue
growth for FY2028, "far above estimates"; the stock rose ~4% [12].

**Why second-order names can 10x when NVIDIA cannot:** the money leaves NVIDIA's P&L and lands in
memory (HBM/NAND), optics, power, cooling, packaging/test and the leased-capacity ("neocloud")
layer, where several companies are 1/100th to 1/1000th of NVIDIA's size and where revenue has been
inflecting 3-8x year on year (see Section 4 for what actually 10x'd in the last 24 months). For context, the SOX index is up ~258% over three years [123] and the optics trio has been extending its surge into September [124].

**Timing.** The window (Sep 2027-Sep 2028) coincides with (a) the first volume year of co-packaged
optics, described in the sources as "limited initial commercial deployments as of 2026, broad volume
production not expected until 2027 or later," with large-scale deployment 2028-2030 [73]; (b) HBM4
production ramps (Counterpoint's 2026 HBM4 share forecast: SK hynix 54%, Samsung 28%, Micron 18%)
[63]; (c) the conversion of multi-GW neocloud contracts into revenue (IREN targets $4bn annualised
run-rate revenue by 31 Dec 2026 [41][43]; Nebius guides $7-9bn ARR exiting 2026 [36]).

## 2. What the market already prices

**Peak-cycle margins are being capitalised as if permanent.** Micron guided fiscal Q4 2026 (reports
30 Sep 2026) to revenue $50.0bn +/- $1.0bn, GAAP gross margin ~86%, non-GAAP EPS $31.00 +/- $1.00
[54][55] (the prior quarter, FQ2, had already nearly tripled revenue y/y [56]). Its market cap is ~$1.15-1.21tn (18-24 Sep 2026) [52][53]. On $31 x 4 = $124 annualised
EPS and a $1,055.64 share price (24 Sep 2026) [51] the P/E is ~8.5x. A single-digit P/E on 86% gross
margins is the textbook peak-cycle signature (memory, 2018 and 2022): the multiple is low precisely
because the market expects the margin to mean-revert. SanDisk is the same trade one layer down: up
"more than 5,200%" since its Feb 2025 spin and ~720% in 2026 alone, market cap ~$214bn, on Gartner's
forecast of a 234% NAND price rise in 2026 [65][66][67][68].

**Optics and interconnect are priced for the 2027 ramp.** Credo: FY2026 revenue $1.34bn (+206%),
Q4 $437m (+157% y/y), guiding to >80% growth in FY2027; market cap $35.2bn (21 Sep 2026) [76][77][78]
- roughly 15x forward sales. Astera Labs: market cap $59-63bn (Sep 2026), TTM revenue ~$1.6bn, Q3 2026
guide $540-560m (~+40% q/q) [80][81][82] - roughly 27x annualised run-rate sales. Lumentum $881.26
(+139% YTD), Coherent $281.86 (+53% YTD), Applied Optoelectronics $105.53 (+203% YTD), all as of the
close before 7 Sep 2026 [69]; NVIDIA invested $2bn each in Lumentum and Coherent in March 2026 with
multi-year purchase agreements [72]. AAOI sold $600m of equity on 24 Aug 2026 (stock -12%) [70].

**Power is priced for a decade of growth.** Bloom Energy's market cap is ~$81bn (mid-Sep 2026) on
2026 revenue guidance of $3.4-3.8bn (2025: $2.02bn, +37%) [110][111] - ~22x sales for a fuel-cell
manufacturer. Vertiv ~$98bn [117]; nVent $24.6bn (3 Sep 2026) [120]; Modine ~$8.3bn [118].

**Neoclouds are priced on contracted, not delivered, revenue - and on other people's balance sheets.**
CoreWeave: market cap $39.2bn (21 Sep 2026) [33] to $49.3bn (undated Sep 2026) [35]; debt $51.6bn vs
cash $5.5bn, debt/equity 10.3 [34]; launched a $3bn convertible plus a share sale on 17 Sep 2026 and
fell [31][32]. Nebius raised >$6bn (a $4.3bn convertible and $2bn of NVIDIA equity), guides 2026
revenue $3.0-3.4bn and capex $20-25bn [36][37][38]. Nscale's S-1 (filed 18 Sep 2026 [26]) targets a $35bn
valuation vs $14.6bn at the last private round; $103bn of contract value of which 85% is Microsoft
(~$43.8bn through 2033) and Anthropic ($44.6bn, an 8 GW West Virginia site for which "Nscale has not
yet secured financing"); H1 2026 revenue $140.6m, net loss ~$1bn; only $2.6bn of the $103bn was active
at end-August; the filing carries going-concern language [22][23][24][25].

**Bubble warnings with teeth (i.e., that identify a specific mechanism):**
- BIS 2026 Annual Report names an AI capex bust and a circular-financing collapse among its top
  three threats to financial stability [16][17].
- Circular financing: chipmakers/hyperscalers take equity in labs or neoclouds, which then commit to
  multi-year purchases from the same funder [20]. Concrete instances in this note: NVIDIA's $2bn in
  Nebius [37], $2bn each in Lumentum and Coherent with purchase agreements [72], and NVIDIA sitting
  "on four sides" of Nscale (shareholder, customer, lease guarantor, sole chip supplier) [21][22].
- Depreciation: Burry argues hyperscalers understate depreciation by ~$176bn over 2026-28 by
  extending GPU useful lives (Meta to 5.5 years, cutting 9M-2025 depreciation by $2.3bn; Alphabet and
  Microsoft at 6 years; Amazon cut some servers back to 5) [27][28][29][30]. The counter-evidence
  offered by the bulls: CoreWeave says expired H100 contracts re-booked at ~95% of the original price;
  Huang cites H100 rental rates up 22% [27]. Neither side's number was independently verified here.
- Credit-chain risk: Quinn Emanuel's client alert traces "GPU depreciation -> tenant default -> data
  centre SPV distress -> ABS impairment" and notes private-credit lenders hold subordinated exposure
  to "collateral that depreciates like smartphones, not buildings" [19].
- Cash-flow strain: Alphabet reported its first negative free cash flow since its 2004 IPO (-$5.9bn);
  hyperscalers are tapping external financing [5]; a 28 Jul 2026 Alphabet report "sparked a sell-off"
  and higher capex scrutiny for Amazon, Meta and Microsoft [6].
- Cramer (27 Jul 2026) compared the circular-financing "frenzy" to the dot-com bubble [18] - noted
  for completeness; a pundit's analogy is not a mechanism.

**What the bears say, in one paragraph.** 2026 is the peak *growth* year for capex (+77%); 2027's
forecast +29% (to ~$935bn) is already an assumed deceleration [1][8]. The marginal buyers of compute
(neoclouds, Nscale, OpenAI-adjacent SPVs) are financed with converts, ABS and vendor equity rather
than operating cash flow [22][32][37]. If a large lab cancels or defers (Nscale's Anthropic contract
is cancellable "tranche by tranche if Nscale delivers late" [21][25]), the loss lands first on the
smallest balance sheets in this note (APLD, IREN, Cipher, TeraWulf, Hut 8), then on the equipment and
optics suppliers whose order books are 6-12 months long. Memory margins at 85%+ have no precedent and
a fixed-price-contract structure [55] that will look very different if spot prices roll over.

$1.3tn all-in 2027 data-centre capex [7]. It would also exceed the combined market value of every
other US-listed company [from memory, verify]. Rejected on implied-cap grounds regardless of
execution; NVIDIA is the benchmark, not the candidate.

### 3.2 Candidate write-ups (only those that survive the implied-cap test on paper)

**IREN.** *What has to be true:* $4bn ARR delivered on schedule (31 Dec 2026), then a second and third
GW contracted with investment-grade counterparties at similar pricing, so that 2028 revenue is in the
$10-15bn range with ~40% EBITDA margins (Nebius's guided margin [36]); the market must then pay
~12-15x revenue, which is what it pays Credo and Marvell today [78][85], not what it pays CoreWeave.
*Catalysts:* 31 Dec 2026 ARR checkpoint [43]; FY27 Q1 report ~Nov 2026; any hyperscaler (Microsoft is
already a customer [43]) signing a multi-year GW-scale deal. *Bear case:* the Aug 2026 miss [44][45]
shows the mining-to-AI transition is expensive; contracted counterparties are start-ups whose own
funding is circular; GPUs are depreciating assets (Sec 2); equity issuance dilutes the 10x.
*Kill criteria:* (1) operating ARR < $3bn at 31 Dec 2026; (2) any announced contract cancellation or
repricing >10% below contract; (3) cumulative share count growth >30% over 12 months without a matching
ARR step-up; (4) a CoreWeave or Nscale credit event (contagion to neocloud financing).
*Evidence quality:* high (8-K [41], 10-K [42]). *Confidence data are correct:* medium (cap figure is from a
secondary article, not a filing).

**Applied Digital.** *What has to be true:* CoreWeave performs on all four halls; APLD contracts an
additional 1.5-2 GW with hyperscalers directly (not via neoclouds); the market values it as a
data-centre REIT/developer at ~20-25x EBITDA [from memory, verify]. Even then $71bn is a stretch: it
is on the order of Equinix's market cap [from memory, verify]. *Catalysts:* new tenant announcements
(the company has said it wants hyperscaler tenants [50]); financing closes on Polaris Forge 2.
*Bear case:* one tenant, which is itself the most levered public neocloud [34]; the 30 Mar 2026 lease
amendment suspending two halls [48] is exactly the kind of event that precedes renegotiation.
*Kill criteria:* (1) CoreWeave misses a lease payment or seeks further amendments; (2) no second
tenant by mid-2027; (3) CoreWeave credit spreads blow out. *Evidence:* high (10-Q). *Confidence:*
medium (cap from TipRanks page, date not explicit).

**POET.** Included only as a lottery ticket that the reader will ask about. *What has to be true:*
the Lumilens framework ($500m over five years) [91] converts into ~$100m/yr revenue by 2028 *and* a
hyperscaler or NVIDIA adopts the optical interposer for CPO, which the sources put at 2028-2030 for
volume [73]. At $12.9bn that is still >100x 2028 sales. *Kill:* Lumilens PO deliveries not shipping by
Q2 2027; any further equity raise before revenue exceeds $10m/quarter. *Evidence:* medium (6-K for
revenue); the $500m framework is a company claim. Confidence: medium. The author's view: a 3-5x
narrative spike is possible in a photonics mania; a fundamental 10x is not.

**Navitas.** *What has to be true:* NVIDIA's 800 V HVDC rack architecture ships in volume in 2027
(Rubin Ultra / Kyber timing [from memory, verify]) and Navitas wins a meaningful share of the
$10-15k/MW GaN content [107]; at 10 GW of 800 V racks that is only $100-150m of GaN revenue, so a 10x
also requires SiC and non-AI recovery. *Kill:* 2026 revenue does not exit above $20m/quarter; NVIDIA
names a different GaN supplier for 800 V. Data is stale (April 2026) - re-price before use. Evidence:
medium. Confidence: low on the current cap.

**Bitcoin-miner conversions (Cipher, TeraWulf, Hut 8).** Public miners sold >32,000 BTC in Q1 2026 to fund the pivot, with >$70bn of HPC contracts signed across the group [102]. The contract sizes ($3.8bn, $12.8bn, $7bn-
$17.7bn) [101][103] relative to what are probably $5-15bn caps [from memory, verify] make this the
sub-sector most likely to contain a 10x *if* Google's credit backstops [101] hold and Fluidstack's
anchor tenants (Anthropic at Hut 8 River Bend [101]) actually take delivery. No market caps or balance
sheets were retrieved, so they cannot be ranked; **priority for a follow-up deep dive** with a fresh

## 5. Where the next inflection might be that is not yet priced

- **Samsung's HBM comeback.** Counterpoint has Samsung's HBM revenue share jumping to 33% in Q2 2026
  from 21% in Q1, with SK hynix losing 8 points in a quarter [61]; yet SK hynix retains ~70% of
  NVIDIA's HBM4 orders [64]. If Samsung qualifies HBM4 at NVIDIA in volume, the share shift is the
  largest unpriced move in memory - but Samsung's cap [NOT FOUND] and it is far too big to 10x.
  Second-order: Korean HBM equipment/materials small caps (Hanmi Semiconductor, etc.) - [NOT FOUND]
  this session; **follow-up**.
- **Co-packaged optics timing.** Volume 2027, scale 2028-30 [73] sits inside the window. Winners
  named by the sources: Lumentum, Coherent (NVIDIA-backed) [72], Credo, Marvell; the only small cap
  with retrieved data is POET (Sec 3). Unpriced risk: NVIDIA bundling optical engines into its own
  switches [73] compresses the merchant transceiver margin that AAOI/Coherent currently enjoy.
- **Inference-cost collapse.** Beneficiaries are demand-elastic (agent workloads, robotics) rather
  than chip vendors. The public inference-silicon pure play, Cerebras, already trades at ~$70-86bn
  [98]; NVIDIA bought Groq's assets for $20bn in Dec 2025 [99]. No GPU rental-price series was
  retrieved [NOT FOUND]; the bull/bear H100 re-booking claims in Sec 2 need a primary source.
- **Physical AI / robotics.** NVIDIA's physical-AI platform is described as ~$10bn annual revenue
  [122]; Figure AI's $39bn Series C (Sep 2025) [121]; Barclays sees ~$200bn humanoid market by 2035
  [121]. Pure plays are private or divisions (Hyundai/Boston Dynamics, Tesla) [122]. Small public
  candidates [NOT FOUND] - assign to the robotics agent.
- **On-device AI / AI PCs.** [NOT FOUND] this session beyond Arm ($294bn [86]) and Ambarella
  ($2.75bn, no revenue data [88]). Needs a search budget.
- **China AI silicon.** Cambricon became the first STAR-market CNY1tn company on 30 Jun 2026; Q1 2026
  revenue CNY2.88bn vs CNY1.11bn, net income CNY1.01bn [95][96][97]. The 10x already happened. Next
  tier (Hygon CNY370.30 early Jul 2026 [95]; MetaX, Biren, Moore Threads IPO status [NOT FOUND]) is
  the unpriced layer for an investor who can hold A-shares/H-shares; access and export-control risk
  are the bear case.
- **New memory (3D DRAM, etc.).** [NOT FOUND] this session.
- **Power delivery at 800 V.** Navitas is the only retrieved small cap [106][107]; the larger
  beneficiaries (Infineon, onsemi, Eaton, Vertiv) are too big to 10x.

## 1. Why this theme could produce a 10x in 1-2 years

### 1.1 Demand: the load-growth story is real and now has hard numbers

- **IEA (2026 "Key Questions on Energy and AI")**: global data-centre electricity use ~485 TWh in
  2025 rising to ~950 TWh in 2030 (~3% of world demand); AI-focused data-centre consumption
  roughly triples over the period; data-centre demand grows ~15%/yr 2024-30, more than four times
  the growth rate of all other sectors combined. [1][2][3][4]
- **PJM 2027/28 Base Residual Auction (results Dec 2025)**: cleared at the FERC cap of
  $333.44/MW-day, total cost ~$16.4bn, second consecutive auction at the cap; the demand forecast
  rose 5,250 MW, almost all data centres; procured reserve margin fell to 14.8%, the lowest PJM has
  recorded; PJM estimates an uncapped clearing price of ~$193,907/MW-yr (~60% above the cap).
  [8][9][10][69] The market monitor attributes $21.3bn of the $47.2bn cleared in the last three
  auctions (45%) to data-centre load, of which ~$6.2bn is for data centres not yet built. [11][12]
- **Gas turbines are the binding constraint on new firm supply.** GE Vernova reported 116 GW of
  gas-power backlog plus slot reservations at end-Q2 2026 (up from 100 GW a quarter earlier and
  83 GW at end-2025); of that only 53 GW is firm equipment backlog, 63 GW is paid slot
  reservations. It is taking reservations for 2031 delivery, and plans ~20 GW/yr now, 24 GW by
  2028, 30 GW by 2030. Siemens Energy: 69 GW firm backlog at 30 Jun 2026. MHI: 35 GW large-frame
  backlog (6 Aug 2026), deliveries 2028-30. Lead times three years or more. [13][14][15]
- **Hyperscaler nuclear offtake (as of 2026, per industry trackers)**: 13+ deals, close to 10 GW
  committed. Microsoft/Constellation: 20-yr agreement to restart Three Mile Island Unit 1 (Crane),
  reported at $16bn, COD now targeted H2 2027. Amazon/Talen: Susquehanna offtake expanded to
  ~2 GW through 2042, plus Amazon-led $700m round in X-energy (up to 12 Xe-100 units).
  Google/Kairos: up to 500 MW across 6-7 units, first reactor targeted 2030. Meta/Constellation:
  1.1 GW, 20 yr, Clinton (announced 3 June [2025]); Meta is reported as the largest cumulative
  procurer at ~6.6 GW across TerraPower, Oklo, Vistra and Constellation deals. [5][6][7][75]
  Note these trackers are secondary sources; the primary announcements were not fetched.

### 1.2 Policy: the regulatory clock has been reset in nuclear's favour

- EO 14300 (23 May 2025) ordered a wholesale revision of NRC regulations: proposed rules within
  9 months, final rules within 18 months (i.e. by ~Nov 2026). [16][74][71][72]
- NRC lists 28 rulemakings in response to Section 5 of the EO, with action on the remaining 27
  (direct-final and proposed rules) by mid-2026. [17][18][20]
- Oklo's Q2 2026 10-Q refers to a "recently finalized Part 53 framework and proposed Part 57
  framework". [23] (Part 57 is a new framework; its content was not verified here.)
- DOE states that four advanced reactors reached criticality by the 4 July 2026 target under the
  reactor pilot programme. Which four is not stated in the results I saw. [19]
- 17 Sep 2026: Oklo +13% and NuScale +10% on a House "data centre power" vote (headline only;
  the bill's content is [NOT FOUND]). [26]

### 1.3 Uranium: term price at a nominal record, largest producer cutting

- Spot U3O8 $89.99/lb (11 Sep 2026); $89.32/lb (1 Sep 2026); up ~20% y/y. Term price
  ~$96-96.50/lb (10 Sep 2026), a nominal all-time high above the 2007 peak of ~$95/lb, and
  $6-7/lb above spot (backwardation of the term curve relative to spot is unusual and signals
  utilities are paying up for security of supply). [46][47][48]
- Kazatomprom cut 2026 nominal capacity from 32,777 tU to 29,697 tU, with guidance
  27,500-29,000 tU (roughly 8 Mlb below the original plan). Russia supplied 93.6% of Kazakhstan's
  sulphuric-acid imports in 2023 and its acid export ban runs to 31 Dec 2026; if not exempted,
  2027 output could fall a further ~3 Mlb (~4%). Formal 2027 guidance not due until Feb 2027.
  [49][50][51][52][47]

### 1.4 The precedent: this sector *did* produce 10x-ers in the last 24 months (see 2.3)

The combination above (load growth + policy + supply squeeze + hyperscaler cheques) is why
capital flowed. The question for Sep 2026 is whether anything is still small enough.

### 2.2 The 2026 drawdown

The Oct 2025 peak was the top for the SMR names: Oklo went from ~$25 at the start of 2025 to an
intraday high ~$194 in Oct 2025, then to ~$63 (as reported in the search summary of [29]; the
exact attribution inside that result set could not be checked) and is ~$42 / down 44% YTD in Sep
2026 [29][26]. NuScale at ~$9.70 [29] is a fraction of its 2025 highs [from memory, verify].
NNE's cap fell from $1.31bn (20 Apr 2026) to $0.98bn (21 Aug 2026) [39]. Eos is down 39% over a
year [67]. Centrus fell 11.9% on the day it raised 2026 guidance and announced the $900m DOE HALEU
award [43], then priced a $500m offering [44]. Read: the market has moved from "any nuclear
narrative" to "show me the contract and the cash".

What is *still* priced in: Oklo at $6-8bn with no revenue until a first reactor operates; NuScale
at $3.5-4.4bn having paid $495m to ENTRA1 for a *non-binding* 6 GW framework (BNP Paribas Exane
estimates NuScale could pay up to $6bn over 15 years under the arrangement) [33][37][34];
Bloom at $63-74bn. None of these is cheap on any conventional measure.

**Common thread of the winners:** (a) tiny starting market caps (roughly $0.3-3bn); (b) a
single-sentence link to AI power demand that a generalist could repeat; (c) a 2025 policy shock
(May 2025 EOs) that made zero-revenue stories fundable; (d) reflexivity: equity raises at rising
prices bought credibility and contracts (Oklo's 424B5s, Centrus's $500m); (e) concentrated in
the Feb-Oct 2025 window. The one gain that *held* (Bloom) is the one with real revenue growth and
index inclusion. The zero-revenue names gave back 50-80% from peak. Implication: the next 10x is
more likely to come from (i) a sub-$1.5bn name that converts narrative into contracted revenue,
or (ii) a fresh listing (fusion) catching a renewed momentum regime, than from re-rating the
already-large SMR names.
